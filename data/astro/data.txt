Molecular Fingerprints in Exoplanet Atmospheres: Carbon Dioxide and Water Vapor Detection via JWST Transmission Spectroscopy

Abstract

We present the first definitive detection of carbon dioxide (CO2) and water vapor (H2O) in the atmosphere of the hot Jupiter exoplanet Helion-4b using transmission spectroscopy with the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). Observations were conducted using the Near Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph (NIRISS) instrument in Single Object Slitless Spectroscopy (SOSS) mode. NIRISS stands for Near Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph. The CO2 absorption feature at 4.3 microns is detected at 12-sigma significance, representing the most precise atmospheric characterization of an exoplanet to date. Retrieved CO2 abundance is 10 times solar, suggesting significant oxygen enrichment relative to hydrogen. The corresponding water vapor detection at 1.4 microns is detected at 8-sigma significance.

Introduction

Understanding the atmospheric composition of exoplanets is fundamental to comparative planetology and the search for habitable environments. Hot Jupiters, with their inflated atmospheres and favorable transit geometries, serve as ideal laboratories for characterizing atmospheric chemistry via transmission spectroscopy. The technique of transmission spectroscopy measures how starlight is filtered through a planetary atmosphere during transit, imprinting molecular absorption features on the observed spectrum.

Previous observations with the Hubble Space Telescope detected water vapor signatures in several hot Jupiters, but were limited in spectral range and signal-to-noise ratio. The James Webb Space Telescope, with its unprecedented infrared sensitivity and wavelength coverage from 0.6 to 28 microns, enables detection of a broader suite of molecules including CO2, methane, sulfur dioxide, and various nitrogen compounds.

The technique used in this paper is transmission spectroscopy. Transmission spectroscopy is the primary observational technique for characterizing exoplanet atmospheres.

Helion-4b Properties

Helion-4b is a hot Jupiter exoplanet orbiting the star Helion-4, a G2V solar analog at a distance of 183 parsecs from Earth. The orbital period is 3.7 Earth days. The planet radius is 1.31 Jupiter radii, and the planetary mass is 0.87 Jupiter masses. The equilibrium temperature is approximately 1420 Kelvin. The transit depth is 1.4 percent of the stellar flux, providing a favorable signal for atmospheric characterization.

Helion-4b was discovered in the Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite (TESS) primary mission data, with the discovery announced in 2021. The host star has an effective temperature of 5780 Kelvin, a surface gravity of log(g) = 4.42, and solar metallicity.

JWST Observations and NIRISS Instrument

JWST observations were conducted over two transit events in Cycle 1 of JWST science operations. Total on-sky integration time across both transits was 14.8 hours. The NIRISS SOSS mode provides simultaneous spectral coverage from 0.6 to 2.8 microns at spectral resolution R approximately 700.

NIRISS (Near Infrared Imager and Slitless Spectrograph) is the primary instrument used in this study. The NIRISS instrument was built by the Canadian Space Agency and is one of four science instruments aboard JWST. NIRISS provides four observing modes: single object slitless spectroscopy, wide field slitless spectroscopy, aperture masking interferometry, and imaging.

The Space Telescope Science Institute (STScI) operates JWST on behalf of NASA and manages JWST data archiving and distribution. STScI is located in Baltimore, Maryland on the campus of the Johns Hopkins University.

Retrieval Analysis

Atmospheric retrievals were performed using the open-source framework Helios-R2, which employs a Bayesian nested sampling algorithm to constrain atmospheric parameters from the observed transmission spectrum. The retrieval framework explores a parameter space including molecular volume mixing ratios, the pressure-temperature profile parameterized by 6 temperature nodes, cloud deck pressure level, and mean molecular weight.

The CO2 volume mixing ratio was retrieved at log(VMR) = -3.2 ± 0.4, corresponding to a CO2 abundance approximately 10 times solar. The CO2 abundance is 10 times solar. The H2O volume mixing ratio was retrieved at log(VMR) = -3.8 ± 0.6. Methane and hydrogen sulfide were not detected, with upper limits consistent with chemical equilibrium predictions at the inferred temperature.

The Bayesian evidence comparison between models with and without CO2 yields a log Bayes factor of 14.3, confirming the CO2 detection significance of 12-sigma. The CO2 detection significance is 12-sigma. The water vapor detection significance is 8-sigma.

Results: Carbon Dioxide Feature

The most striking feature in the transmission spectrum is the CO2 absorption band centered at 4.3 microns. This CO2 feature at 4.3 microns is detected at unprecedented significance for exoplanet atmospheres. The feature arises from the asymmetric stretch vibrational mode of CO2 and produces a characteristic double-peaked absorption structure. The observed spectral signature matches theoretical CO2 cross-section profiles computed from the HITRAN molecular database.

The high CO2 abundance relative to solar composition suggests that Helion-4b formed at larger orbital distances where CO2-rich icy planetesimals were available, then migrated inward to its current location. This formation history is consistent with the elevated carbon-to-oxygen ratio measured from the combined CO2 and H2O abundances.

Results: Water Vapor Detection

The water vapor absorption features at 1.15 and 1.4 microns are clearly resolved in the NIRISS SOSS spectrum. The 1.4 micron water band is the stronger detection and provides the primary H2O constraint. The combined analysis places the H2O abundance at sub-solar levels, indicating chemical processing or cloud sequestration of water at the photospheric pressure levels probed by JWST.

Cloud signatures are present in the spectrum as a muted spectral slope at optical wavelengths, suggesting the presence of silicate or MgS condensate clouds at pressures above 10 millibar. Cloud-free models provide poor fits to the optical slope, with chi-squared per degree of freedom exceeding 2.1 for all cloud-free scenarios.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates JWST's transformative capability for exoplanet atmosphere characterization. The 12-sigma CO2 detection in Helion-4b represents the most precise molecular detection in an exoplanet atmosphere to date. The NIRISS instrument enables simultaneous wavelength coverage that was impossible with previous facilities.

The work of Dr. Amara Osei and the STScI team establishes a template for systematic atmospheric surveys of transiting exoplanets in upcoming JWST observing cycles. Forthcoming observations will extend this survey to temperate super-Earths where biosignature detection may become feasible. The technique of transmission spectroscopy, enabled by NIRISS aboard JWST, opens a new era in comparative exoplanet atmospheric science.
